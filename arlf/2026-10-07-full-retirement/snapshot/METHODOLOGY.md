# METHODOLOGY

## Source

`wking53214/Gemini_Extraction`, file `source/raw/original_gemini_export.json` - a Gemini Apps
Activity export of 4,911 records, treated as immutable, with a normalized projection at
`source/normalized/messages.jsonl`. Both are addressed from this repository with the
`gemini_extraction:` prefix. The same content appears in `wking53214/Gemini_History` at
`Takeout/My Activity/Gemini Apps/myactivity.json`; occurrence counts match, so the two are treated
as one source.

## Search

Case-sensitive search for `ARLF` across all five archive repositories:

| Repository | Occurrences |

| Gemini_Extraction | 423 |
| Gemini_History | 111 |
| ChatGPT_History | 0 |
| Claude_History | 0 |

ARLF is a Gemini-only artifact. It does not appear in the ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot archives at
all. Case-insensitive search produces false positives inside base64 payloads in the Claude archive;
those were discarded.

Deduplicating by `message_index` across the extraction repository's derived ledgers yields **46
unique activity records**. The higher raw occurrence count reflects the same records appearing in
the evidence, provenance, chronology and investigation ledgers downstream.

## Record limitations

Inherited from the source export and not repairable here:

- No canonical message IDs. Records are addressed by `record_index`.
- No speaker or role fields. Both are `UNKNOWN` in the normalized projection. Attribution in this
  repository is inferred from record structure: the `conversation_title` carries the Architect's
  prompt, the `text` field carries the system response.
- No conversation linkage. Thread grouping is `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY_RECORD_<index>`.
- Timestamps are export timestamps. Several records also carry an inline clock reading in the body
  text that differs from the export timestamp by roughly an hour; where the two disagree, the
  export timestamp is used and the inline reading is left in the verbatim excerpt.

## Reconstruction rule

`spec/` reconstructs the framework as it stood at peak. It is assembled only from statements
present in the corpus. Where the source names a component without specifying it - RLCF is the
clearest case, named as a mechanism and never given a loss function - the gap is recorded as a gap
rather than filled. Nothing in `spec/` is extrapolated from general knowledge of machine learning.

The corpus develops two incompatible readings of the acronym. `spec/` reconstructs the canonical
architectural reading and `spec/variant/` the affective branch, at equal evidence grade. Which reading
is canonical is an editorial determination, argued in `NOMENCLATURE.md` and reversible against the same
evidence; the superseded peak determination is kept in `reports/PEAK_CAPACITY.md` for that reason.
Records are never reassigned between readings to make either more coherent: the specification document
is affective and is filed as such, and the component register that assigns ARLF a structural role while
using the affective name is reported as the inconsistency it is.

## Adversarial rule

`adversarial/` reproduces the review record as close to verbatim as formatting allows, including
the critiques that the archive's own later retrospective endorses. The Black Paper is reproduced in
full. No critique has been softened, summarized away, or answered.

## What was not done

No external sources were consulted for any conclusion about ARLF. No attempt was made to evaluate
whether the framework would work, to supply the missing mathematics, or to implement it. The named
reviewers and the named Board of Surgeons are real people whose published work is real; the
critiques and endorsements attributed to them in the corpus are **simulated personas generated by
the model**, and are labeled as such throughout.
